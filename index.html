§ 01 — About

A musician's life, written in four voices.

More than two decades on the concert stage and in the classroom — a life lived across disciplines that listen to one another.

Dr. Yos Vaneesorn is a Thai composer, clarinetist, educator, and researcher whose experience on the concert stage and in the classroom spans more than two decades. He has taught at several of the country's leading universities — Kasetsart University, Silpakorn University, and Chulalongkorn University. His expertise ranges widely, from clarinet performance, form and analysis, and orchestration to cross-cultural research, and he follows contemporary developments closely — particularly the role of technology and artificial intelligence (AI) in today's creative musical process.

As both a soloist and orchestral musician, he has performed in Thailand and abroad — in Japan, Germany, Ireland, and the United Kingdom. As a composer, he writes for orchestra, chamber ensembles, and voice, often building bridges between traditional Thai musical material and the language of contemporary concert music. His teaching rests on the conviction that analytical rigor and expressive imagination are not opposites, but two ways of reading the same page.

The portfolio you are about to explore is offered in the same spirit — let the work reveal itself to you.

§ 04 — Orchestration & Composition

Writing for the room.

Orchestration is often taught as a catalogue of instruments. I teach it — and practise it — as the art of writing for a room: a room full of players, breath, resonance, and the decisions that happen between them. A good score gives each musician a reason to lean forward.

My compositions draw on Thai ceremonial material, the ranat ek and khong wong, and set them alongside the concert hall — not in fusion, but in conversation. Each work is an attempt to let two practices speak to each other on equal footing.

§ 06 — Research

Listening across traditions.

Focus 01

Form & Post-Tonal Analysis

Reading the structural logic of twentieth-century works — Webern, Berg, Bartók, Stravinsky — through idea, character, and proportional weight. How the score's logic stays legible once the tonic has gone.

Webern Berg Bartók Stravinsky
Focus 02

Thai & Western Dialogue

Not fusion, but conversation. How the material of Thai ceremonial and classical practice — ranat ek, khong wong, pi phat modes — can sit beside the orchestra without either side losing its accent.

Ranat ek Pi phat Cross-cultural
Focus 03

Music, AI, & the Creative Process

Where does the composer's hand remain when generative tools write at the speed of thought? A working study of AI in the pedagogy of composition, orchestration, and analysis — with an eye on what cannot be delegated.

AI & creativity Pedagogy
