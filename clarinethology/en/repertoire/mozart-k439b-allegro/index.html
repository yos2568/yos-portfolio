At a glance

Clarinet trio (originally three basset horns). Upper-intermediate. Movement I Allegro rewards blend, Classical elegance, and conversational equality of parts more than solo virtuosity. Excellent studio and chamber-class repertoire.

Composer & composition

Composer & the K. 439b divertimenti

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (27 January 1756, Salzburg – 5 December 1791, Vienna) left a chamber legacy that includes the five Divertimenti K. 439b (also catalogued in relation to the basset-horn divertimenti). These works were written for three basset horns — instruments with a darker, more homogeneous colour than a mixed clarinet trio. Modern performances often use three clarinets or two clarinets plus bass clarinet; editors such as the Trio di Clarone (Sabine Meyer, Wolfgang Meyer, Reiner Wehle) have been central to the modern performing tradition on original instruments.

Divertimento No. 4 in B-flat major opens with an Allegro that models Classical clarity: balanced phrases, transparent texture, and conversational exchange among three equal voices. The pedagogical value is ensemble — matching tone concept, Classical phrase-shaping with modest tools (speed and dynamics), and resisting Romantic weight.

Performance notes

Classical elegance

Never romanticize; keep ornaments and rubato modest. Aim for the mellow, slightly darker colour of the basset-horn original so three parts fuse into one sound.

  • Fabian Hügli – Phrasing
  • Trio di Clarone notes

Difficulty & suitability

Upper-intermediate. Technical demands are moderate compared with Rossini or Weber concertos; the difficulty is musical and ensemble-based — blend, Classical phrasing, and equal-voice chamber discipline. Ideal for advanced intermediate high-school chamber groups and college studio classes. Teachers can use it to train listening, balance, and historical tone concept.
